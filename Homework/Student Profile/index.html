Three Technologies

Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the field of building computer systems that can perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence — like understanding language, recognizing patterns, making decisions, or learning from experience.

In short: AI = machines that can learn from data and make predictions or decisions, instead of just following fixed, pre-written rules.

Core idea: Instead of a programmer explicitly coding every rule ("if X, then Y"), an AI system is trained on data and learns patterns itself, then applies what it learned to new, unseen situations.

Common forms today:

Machine Learning (ML) — systems that improve at a task by learning from examples/data
Deep Learning — ML using neural networks (inspired loosely by the brain) for complex tasks like image or speech recognition
Large Language Models (LLMs) — like the one you're talking to now, trained on huge amounts of text to understand and generate language

Quantum Computing

Quantum Computing is a way of computing that uses the principles of quantum mechanics (how particles behave at the subatomic level) to process information very differently from regular computers.

In short:Quantum computers use qubits instead of classical bits, letting them explore many possible solutions at once — making them potentially much faster for specific types of problems.

Core Idea: A regular bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can be in a mix of both states at the same time (superposition), and qubits can be linked together (entanglement) so their states affect each other instantly — even at a distance. This lets quantum computers process certain calculations in parallel in a way classical computers can't.

Augmentated Reality(AR)/Virtual Reality(VR)

AR (Augmented Reality) adds digital elements on top of the real world; VR (Virtual Reality) replaces the real world entirely with a simulated one.

Core idea:

AR — you still see your actual surroundings, but digital objects/info are overlaid on them (through a phone screen or glasses)
VR — you're fully immersed in a computer-generated environment, usually via a headset that blocks out the real world
